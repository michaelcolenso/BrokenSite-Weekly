# BrokenSiteweekly — Verified lead product plan

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Goal:** Deliver a trusted shortlist of active local businesses with website problems an agency can explain and fix.

Buyers are paying to save prospecting time and avoid embarrassing pitches. The next milestone is ten leads a buyer considers worth pursuing.

## Paid lead standard

A paid lead must pass three gates:

1. **Viable business:** Active, independent, local, and relevant to the buyer. Exclude closed businesses and national chains.
2. **Meaningful problem:** A verified issue affects customers' ability to access the site, use an important page, contact the business, or complete a key action.
3. **Evidence:** A specific URL, a plain-English description, a verification date, and supporting evidence where useful.

Use a score to order qualifying leads. Admission to the paid list should depend on these gates.

## Why the scoring-fix plan is insufficient

Removing incidental signals and excluding chains would reduce noise. A reason-based filter still needs verification:

- An old copyright year can appear on a usable website.
- WordPress, Wix, marketing tags, and missing email addresses do not establish a customer problem.
- A parked domain can belong to a closed business.
- A scanner timeout or access block can occur while the site works for customers.
- An HTTP-only site may offer a small security fix rather than a valuable rebuild.

Put uncertain cases into review. Keep general rebuild candidates separate from verified website problems. Do not infer a business's budget or willingness to buy from a technical signal.

## Next steps

### 1. Produce a small verified sample

Use the existing 228-row Seattle/Tacoma corpus as the candidate pool. Its reported scan results are starting evidence, not proof that every flagged site is broken.

Manually verify the business and website problem for 10–15 strong candidates. Record the business, website, metro, trade, issue, customer impact, evidence, verification date, and basis for considering the business active.

Record why rejected candidates fail. If the corpus cannot produce ten strong leads, report that result rather than lowering the standard to fill the list.

**Deliverable:** A sample lead list and a short rejection log.

### 2. Test usefulness with buyers

Have 3–5 potential freelancer or agency buyers review the sample. Learn which leads they would pursue, which they reject, what is missing, and whether they will pay for another batch.

Seek a paid trial and evidence of repeat demand. Treat compliments as feedback; record purchasing and usage decisions.

**Deliverable:** Buyer feedback and a decision on whether to produce another batch.

### 3. Define the qualification standard

Turn verification results and buyer feedback into clear inclusion rules, rejection rules, and a review bucket. Keep a labelled sample for checking future output.

**Deliverable:** A short qualification checklist and examples of accepted, rejected, and uncertain candidates.

### 4. Automate what proves useful

Automate discovery, rejection, and evidence collection according to the qualification standard. Apply the same admission rules to every paid output, including local exports and subscriber delivery.

Keep scoring changes and chain exclusions as supporting cleanup. Choose the implementation after the manual workflow and buyer feedback establish what matters.

**Deliverable:** A focused engineering plan grounded in the verified sample.

## Success and cadence

Track confirmed problems, the fraction of leads buyers would pursue, willingness to pay for another batch, fresh qualifying supply, and verification time.

Commit to a weekly subscription once one metro can repeatedly supply useful new opportunities at a sustainable cost. If the supply supports a monthly report or a one-off metro pack better, use that format.

## Scope for this validation pass

Use the existing Seattle/Tacoma candidates. No new Maps scrape, new metros, automated business outreach, or broad scanner rewrite. Do not claim the current database has been verified until the sample work is complete.
